Long before I built the `herdianurdin.my.id` blog, newly released this October 2026, I navigated the dark alleys of internet monetization. A long discussion with a college friend in 2023—right before I graduated—opened my eyes to the brutal reality behind the Google Play Console.

The conversation was triggered by Google's new rules in November 2023 that began suffocating personal _publishers_. Google mandated a closed testing phase involving 20 devices for 14 consecutive days before an app could be released. To me, it was a quality assurance standard. To my friend, it was a minor apocalypse.

## The Golden Era of Instant Cash and Dirty ASO Tactics

My friend was a representation of the "players" who exploit system loopholes. He shared that before those strict rules dropped, the Play Console was a goldmine for anyone who understood _App Store Optimization_ (ASO). It didn't matter how terrible your app was; as long as you mastered the search algorithms and crammed in as much ad mediation as possible, you could pocket tens of millions of rupiah every month.

The ASO tactics he taught were highly pragmatic, manipulative, and utilized specific tools:

1. **Play Store Search Engine Exploitation:** For fast, _real-time_ research, he didn't rely on third-party tools. He broke down core keywords directly in the Play Store search bar to gather _autocomplete_ data.
2. **Algorithmic Cross-Testing:** Because Google's algorithm personalizes search results for each user, data from a single phone is invalid. He would borrow phones from friends or his parents (using different Google accounts/emails) to cross-test keyword rankings and gather objective data before cannibalizing competitors' titles and descriptions.
3. **Metric Monitoring via AppBrain:** Tools like AppBrain were still used, but strictly for passive metric monitoring, as their data updates were considered too slow for rapid execution.
4. **Seasonal Event Categories:** Riding the wave of trends like "August 17th Independence Twibbons" or holiday greetings to instantly crack the top search nominations.
5. **Account Farming with Virtual Machines:** Because these "trash" apps usually survived for less than a year before being _banned_, the solution was to constantly switch and buy Console accounts, harvest the hype, and run. To evade detection, these accounts were isolated using _Virtual Machines_ (VM).

## Bloodsport Competition: Web vs. Apps

This is a dirty business. The competition is no longer about who has the cleanest code, but who can take down their opponents the fastest. Tactics range from deploying fake 1-star reviews using bot accounts, to intentionally committing _click-bombing_ (mass-clicking ads) on competitor apps so their AdMob accounts get hit with an _ad limit_ or a permanent ban.

As someone who started a career as a _blogger_, I compared this to the _website_ ecosystem. In reality, the _web_ world isn't much different. Back in 2020, my old blog, _herdaynote.com_, once reached an income of Rp50,000 per day. Unfortunately, a malicious competitor sent a flood of garbage _traffic_, causing my revenue to be algorithmically restricted.

Even worse, I had another friend (a minimarket employee and _tech enthusiast_) who raked in Rp40 million a month in his first three months just by creating fake sites (MFA - _Made for AdSense/Adsterra_). These sites manipulated _Search Engine_ algorithms; when clicked, instead of showing an article, visitors were bombarded with _pop-up_ ads.

A logical question arose: _If the rules are so strict, why are many massive apps or games that clearly violate policies (like disguised gambling) kept alive by Google?_
The answer is simple. These apps have giant installation numbers that contribute to massive cash flow. Ultimately, it’s a business, and corporations don't want to cut off their own profit sources.

## Choosing the Quiet Path: A Halal Digital Legacy

Seeing all the instant "success" of my friends, I could have easily jumped in. However, I chose a different path.

I built this main Play Console account using **my real name**. It serves as a digital footprint and a showcase of responsibility for the _software engineering_ knowledge I have acquired. I want to build things that are useful. The revenue from AdMob is just a bonus to keep operations running. I am not money-crazed, especially if that money destroys my sanity and peace of mind.

My survival strategy is highly orthodox yet sustainable:

- **Legacy Device Optimization:** I ensure my apps run smoothly and have small file sizes. I design and test them on an obsolete phone (a Redmi 5A with 2GB of RAM). If it runs flawlessly there, it will fly on modern phones.
- **Humane UI/UX:** No deceptive ad placements (_accidental clicks_).
- **Sensitive Content Blocking:** Although ad networks sometimes slip in garbage ads, I strictly filter them using the _AdMob Blocking Controls_ to reject gambling, pornography, and scam ads to ensure my income remains _halal_.

My growth from 2023 to the present might not be exponential. There are no monthly revenues in the tens of millions. However, my digital assets remain stable (_sustain_), relevant, and continuously generate income without the anxiety of waiting for a _suspend email_ from Google.

Life in this world is short. I don't want the internet algorithm to remember me as a search engine manipulator or a greedy _developer_ who built fake apps. I want to be remembered through the _tools_ I developed to make other people's lives easier.
